# Elicitation Techniques Reference

Deep-dive into techniques for gathering software requirements.

## 1. Interviews
One-on-one conversations with stakeholders.
- **When to use:** Early in the project, when dealing with complex or sensitive topics, or when stakeholders are geographically distributed.
- **Pros:** Deep insights, allows for immediate follow-up questions, builds rapport.
- **Cons:** Time-consuming, potential for interviewer bias, single perspective per session.
- **Tips:** Prepare open-ended questions. Record sessions (with permission) to focus on the conversation rather than note-taking.

## 2. Workshops
Collaborative group sessions with multiple stakeholders.
- **When to use:** When cross-functional alignment is needed, resolving conflicting requirements, defining high-level scope.
- **Pros:** Builds consensus, fast requirement gathering, uncovers dependencies early.
- **Cons:** Hard to schedule, dominant personalities can overshadow quiet participants.
- **Tips:** Use a strong facilitator. Employ visual aids (whiteboards, sticky notes).

## 3. Observation (Shadowing)
Watching users perform their tasks in their natural environment.
- **When to use:** When replacing an existing system, or when users struggle to articulate their workflow (tacit knowledge).
- **Pros:** Uncovers unstated "workarounds" and true operational pain points.
- **Cons:** Observer effect (users act differently when watched), time-intensive.
- **Tips:** Ask "why" during observation, but avoid interrupting flow.

## 4. Document Analysis
Reviewing existing documentation, manuals, legacy code, or logs.
- **When to use:** Migrating legacy systems, regulatory projects, or when SMEs are unavailable.
- **Pros:** Objective baseline, doesn't require stakeholder time.
- **Cons:** Documentation is often outdated or incomplete.
- **Tips:** Cross-reference findings with user interviews.

## 5. Prototyping / Wireframing
Creating visual representations of the proposed solution.
- **When to use:** When UI/UX is critical, or stakeholders have trouble visualizing abstract requirements.
- **Pros:** Tangible feedback, clarifies misunderstandings quickly, validates assumptions.
- **Cons:** Can set false expectations about development speed (looks "done").
- **Tips:** Keep prototypes low-fidelity initially to focus on flow rather than aesthetics.

## 6. Surveys / Questionnaires
Distributing structured questions to a large audience.
- **When to use:** Gathering quantitative data, broad user base, validating hypotheses at scale.
- **Pros:** Scalable, cost-effective, objective data.
- **Cons:** Low response rates, no ability to ask follow-up questions on ambiguous answers.
- **Tips:** Keep it short. Use clear, closed-ended questions with a few optional open-ended fields.

## 7. User Journey Mapping
Visualizing the steps a user takes to accomplish a goal.
- **When to use:** Designing new customer experiences, identifying friction points in multi-step processes.
- **Pros:** Highlights emotional highs/lows, ensures holistic system view.
- **Cons:** Can become overly complex if not bounded.
- **Tips:** Focus on the primary "happy path" first, then map critical exceptions.
